Project 02
UVM ME2990 Mechatronics · "Sustainability Showdown" Competition
Autonomous Recycling Robot Competition

Semester-long team project to design, build, and program a fully autonomous robot for a 3-stage recycling competition. The robot had to push bottles into collection zones, load bins, and traverse a ramp — all without human input after the start button. The design evolved significantly from an initial claw-based concept to a simpler, more robust plow-and-cup-holder mechanism that proved more reliable under competition conditions.

Designed laser-cut wood frame in AutoCAD (bottom base, top shelf, trapezoid supports, motor mounts, custom 3D-printed wheels) — all custom fabricated in the Fab Lab; validated motor torque sufficient for ramp climbing (22.56 N·mm motor vs. 15.39 N·mm required)
Stage 1: open-loop timed differential drive sequence to push 3 bottles from pick-up zone to drop-off — tuned turn timing (slow left at 350ms → fast left at 4600ms → both at 100 PWM) for repeatable navigation
Stage 2: open-loop straight drive into nearest bin, with cup holders loaded above plow — guaranteed 2 bottles scored per run without complex sensing
Stage 3: ultrasonic sensor-driven back-and-forth ramp traversal — direction flipped each time HC-SR04 detected bin at 55cm threshold, accumulating max crossing points; P-control motor loop maintained consistent speed
Wrote 3 independent Arduino state machine programs (switch-case event-driven architecture) for the 3 competition stages — ultrasonic event checker, button debounce, PWM motor control via 2 H-bridges, encoder interrupts

Project 03
UVM ME5520 Computational Solid Mechanics · Final Project · Spring 2026
Nip Roller Structural Validation
— ABAQUS Contact FEA

A prior attempt to ideate and implement a small intermediate roller on the Viatris line had failed due to plastic bending concerns. This FEA study directly addressed that concern: does the improved design (roller seated on the large anvil) eliminate plastic deformation?

Built a 3-part ABAQUS assembly: rigid anvil, 3D deformable Al 6061 roller (6.35mm dia., 300mm length), and rubber coating — surface-to-surface explicit contact with "hard" normal and frictionless tangential behavior
Applied 20N concentrated loads at each roller end via coupling constraints simulating bearing locations; Encastre BC on anvil; displacement-only constraint on roller (vertical motion toward anvil)
Performed mesh convergence study — strain energy converged at ~840 hex-dominated elements; explicit dynamic step with mass scaling factors 1,000–100,000
Max Von Mises stress: 29.03 MPa (FEA) vs. 23.87 MPa (analytical beam theory, σ = Mc/I) — ~20% difference, both far below Al 6061 yield strength (~270 MPa), confirming no plastic deformation
Conclusion validated the redesigned roller for proof-of-concept line operation — the supported-on-anvil geometry eliminated the cantilever failure mode observed in previous attempts
